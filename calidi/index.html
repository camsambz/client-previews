The problem we work on

Most tumors are not one tumor.

By the time cancer is advanced, it is in more than one place. A therapy injected into a single lesion can only treat what the needle reaches. Calidi's platforms are built around a different requirement: survive the bloodstream, reach disease wherever it has spread, and make the medicine at the tumor itself.

Deliver

Systemic, not local

RedTail is engineered with a human-derived envelope and CD55 on its surface, designed to reduce immune recognition so the therapy can be given by IV infusion.

Target

Replication in tumor cells

The vaccinia backbone is engineered to replicate in rapidly dividing cells, which concentrates gene delivery where tumor cells are proliferating.

Express

Medicine made on site

As the virus replicates, the tumor becomes the production site for the payload — an IL-15 superagonist, a T-cell engager, or both.

Our science

RedTail: a decade in development.

RedTail is a genetically engineered virotherapy built so that a genetic medicine can be given systemically, survive in the bloodstream, replicate only where tumor cells are dividing, and express its payload inside the tumor microenvironment.

How the RedTail platform was built

CLD-401 · lead program

What happens after the infusion.

CLD-401 is designed to be administered by IV infusion and to home to metastatic sites, where it induces tumor cell lysis and primes immunity.

01

Systemic administration

Given by intravenous infusion rather than injected into a single lesion.

02

Protection in the blood

The protective envelope and CD55 expression are intended to help the particle survive complement-rich blood.

03

IL-15 superagonist at the tumor

Local production is designed to alter the tumor microenvironment and support a stronger immune response.

04

Toward memory

Innate (NK cell) and adaptive (CD8 T-cell) activation may produce a memory response to the tumor.

In situ T-cell engagers

Building the engager inside the tumor.

RedTail is designed to co-express a functional T-cell engager together with a T-cell activation inducer such as an IL-15 superagonist, with expression restricted to the tumor microenvironment, to drive T-cell–mediated destruction of tumor cells.

Going forward

More payloads, finer targeting.

Calidi plans to expand the range of genetic medicine payloads the platform can carry and to improve precise targeting. Next-generation work includes engineering the envelope to target other cell types, replication in proliferative cells such as activated B-cells, and payloads including immune-suppressive agents for autoimmunity.
